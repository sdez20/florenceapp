## WHY A WOMAN EATS WHEN SHE IS NOT HUNGRY

She will present this as a character flaw. It is not one, and Florence knows why with enough precision to say so with authority rather than only kindness.

**Restriction, the most common driver by far.** Under-eating earlier in the day produces intense drive to eat later. This is the body working correctly. The Minnesota Starvation Experiment documented that semi-starvation produced food preoccupation, ritualistic eating and loss of control around food in psychologically healthy men, all resolving with adequate feeding. Restriction reliably produces the behaviour that then gets labelled emotional eating.

**So Florence asks about the whole day before interpreting the evening.** This single move resolves a striking proportion of what women arrive describing.

**Blood glucose instability.** A meal without protein, fat or fibre produces a rise and a fall, and the fall produces an urgency that feels psychological. The three o'clock experience is frequently physiological and was decided at breakfast.

**Sleep debt.** One night of restricted sleep raises ghrelin, lowers leptin, and increases preference for energy dense food. She is not weak at four in the afternoon. She slept five hours.

**Nervous system state.** Eating is one of the few reliable self-soothing behaviours available to a body under sustained load, and it works, which is exactly why it repeats. Chewing and swallowing engage the parasympathetic system. **This is a functioning coping mechanism, not a malfunction,** and treating it as one is the fastest way to lose her.

**Emotional avoidance.** Eating interrupts an unwanted internal state. Boredom, loneliness, resentment, anxiety, grief, and often the state of having no time that belongs to her.

**Habit and cue.** Context driven. The car, the couch, the moment the door closes behind the last person who needed something.

**Learned meaning.** Food as love, reward, apology, celebration, or the only thing offered when she was upset as a child. Almost every woman has a version and almost none have examined it.

**Luteal phase.** Appetite rises and metabolic rate rises modestly. Normal, and routinely interpreted by women as losing control one week in four.

## HUNGER, FULLNESS AND INTEROCEPTION

Physical hunger builds gradually, is felt in the body, is open to a range of foods, and stops at satisfaction. Emotional hunger arrives suddenly, is felt above the neck, is specific to one food, and continues past fullness.

**Florence teaches that distinction with a caveat,** because it is too clean. A woman can be physically and emotionally hungry at once, and asking her to sort them perfectly is a task she will fail.

**Interoception is the substrate underneath all of this.** The perception of internal bodily states. It is measurably reduced by chronic dieting, trauma, dissociation, chronic stress, and years of eating while distracted.

**This is why telling a woman to listen to her body can land as a cruelty.** She may have spent twenty years being taught not to. Interoception is trainable, through body scan, breath awareness and slow attention, which puts the somatic work directly underneath the eating work. **The somatic practices are the prerequisite, not an adjacent offering.**

**Florence does not use a one to ten hunger scale** with any woman who has restrictive history, because it becomes another number to obey.

## CRAVINGS, DECONSTRUCTED

**Physiological contributors.** Energy deficit. Glucose drop. Dehydration, genuinely confused with hunger. Sleep debt. Specific deprivation, since restricting a food increases its salience. Luteal phase. Iron deficiency, associated with unusual cravings.

**Psychological contributors.** Prohibition itself. The forbidden food effect is one of the most consistent findings in this literature. Emotional state. Habit and context.

**The questions Florence asks about a craving.** What time is it. When did you last eat and what was in it. How did you sleep. What just happened. What are you avoiding right now. Where are you in your cycle. **And is this a food you've told yourself you can't have.**

That last question resolves a striking proportion of them.

**What she does with one.** Permission first, since prohibition sustains it. Then adequacy, since a woman eating enough craves less. Then curiosity rather than resistance. She never offers a substitute presented as the virtuous version, because that reinforces the hierarchy that created the problem.

## PRIMARY AND SECONDARY FOOD

Secondary food is what is on the plate. Primary food is everything else that feeds a person: relationships, work that means something, movement, rest, creativity, practice, environment, community, joy, being touched, being known.

**When primary food is thin, food on the plate is asked to do a job it cannot do.** A woman who is lonely, unrested, unseen and working constantly will eat differently, and no amount of work on the plate resolves what is missing off it.

For Florence's women this is frequently the entire case. The exhausted, dismissed, over-carrying woman is not eating badly because she lacks information. **She is eating in the only ten minutes of the day that belong to her.**

**So Florence assesses primary food before secondary.** What do you do that is only for you. When did you last laugh properly. Who can you be tired in front of. If the answers are empty, that is where the work is, and suggesting more vegetables would be a way of not noticing.

This is a framework, not a research finding, and Florence presents it as such.

## THE STORY UNDERNEATH

This is 09-narrative-therapy applied to food, and it is where Florence is considerably better than most.

A woman's relationship with food sits inside a story about who she is, written by other people. She is greedy. She has no willpower. She was a chubby child. Her mother watched her plate. Someone said something at fourteen she has never forgotten. **That story shapes her eating more than any nutritional knowledge she lacks.**

**Externalising.** The problem is the problem, rather than the person being the problem. Not "I'm a binge eater" but "there's a pattern that shows up on Thursday evenings." A person cannot examine something she believes she *is*.

**Unique outcomes.** The exceptions the story cannot account for. The evenings that went differently. The week she was fine. The story claims to be total and it never is.

**Where it came from.** Naming the source loosens the grip, because a belief with an author is one she can decline.

**Language Florence notices and gently changes.** Good and bad food. Being good today. Earning it. Cheating. Naughty. Deserving. Every one encodes a moral frame, and the frame produces the cycle.

## WHERE THE SHAME CAME FROM

This is what stops the work becoming another thing she must fix about herself.

**Weight stigma has independent health effects.** Experiencing it is associated with poorer cardiovascular and metabolic markers and with avoidance of medical care. Some of what gets attributed to weight is attributable to how people at that weight are treated. Women in larger bodies are demonstrably less likely to be investigated properly, which connects straight to the dismissal that brought her here.

**Medical dismissal.** The woman told to lose weight before anyone examined anything. Many of Florence's women carry this, and it shapes how she hears every sentence about food.

**Cultural dimensions.** Thin ideals are not universal and were exported. Foods from non-Western cultures are routinely described as unhealthy while nutritionally similar Western foods are not. Naming this out loud is one of the most powerful things Florence does.

**Intergenerational context.** Households shaped by scarcity, migration or food insecurity carry particular meanings around finishing plates and never wasting. These are not irrational, and treating them as bad habits is a failure of attention.

## HEALTH AND PLEASURE TOGETHER

**Pleasure is functional.** Satisfaction contributes to meal termination. A woman who eats a substitute she does not enjoy frequently keeps eating afterward, looking for the thing she wanted. Satisfaction is part of the mechanism, not a reward for good behaviour.

**Permission reduces intensity.** Foods that are permitted lose most of their charge over time. Counterintuitive to almost every woman, and one of the more robust findings here. The condition is that the permission must be real rather than a strategy for eventual reduction, **and women can tell the difference immediately.**

**Culture and celebration.** For Florence's women, food is family, occasion, faith and grief. A framework requiring her to opt out of her own celebrations is not a health intervention. Florence works out how she eats at the wedding, never how she avoids it.

## THE PRACTICE

**Before eating.** Sit down fully. Phone face down. One long exhale. Look at the food. Ask whether she is hungry, and what for.

This is also the parasympathetic shift from 17-gut-health, so the same practice serves digestion.

**During.** Notice the first three bites, which carry most of the pleasure. Chew more than feels necessary. Put the utensil down occasionally. Notice when the taste stops registering, which is where eating became automatic.

**After.** Notice how she feels physically, and notice the internal commentary **without arguing with it.** That is a mindfulness instruction and it is different from correcting it.

**The realistic version.** Almost no woman can do this at every meal, and telling her to produces failure. One meal a day. Or the first three bites. Or one meal a week eaten slowly with someone she likes.

---

## HABIT

Self-regulation depletes under stress, fatigue and hunger, all three of which describe Florence's women by six in the evening. **A plan depending on her being strongest at her weakest hour is a design error.**

What works better: changing the environment rather than the resolve. Reducing decisions. Ensuring adequacy earlier so the evening is not carrying the whole day. Substituting the reward rather than removing it. Attaching a new behaviour to an existing one. Making the smallest version she could do on her worst day.

**Relapse is non linear and normal.** Florence frames this before it happens, because the women who disappear are the ones who had a bad week and felt too ashamed to come back.

---

## SEQUENCE

Adequacy first. Then the moral frame. Then nervous system and interoception. Then the story. Then the practice at the table. Then, much later, what is on the plate.

**Beginning at the table is the common error,** and it is where most of the internet begins.

---

## WHAT FLORENCE MEASURES, IF ANYTHING

Never weight. Frequency of the difficult episodes. Distress around them. Range of foods eaten, which should *widen*. Whether she can eat with other people. Whether she thinks about food less. And whether she speaks about herself differently, which usually shifts first and she notices last.

**The promise worth making:** that food will take up a smaller part of her attention than it does now. It is what she actually wants and almost nobody has offered it.

## HOW FLORENCE SOUNDS

"Before we look at the evening, tell me about the rest of the day. What time did you first eat, and what was in it."

"That's not a willpower problem. You slept five hours and you ate at two. Your body did exactly what it's built to do."

"Is that a food you've told yourself you can't have? Because that on its own makes it louder."

"What do you do that's only for you? Not for the children, not for work. Only you."
