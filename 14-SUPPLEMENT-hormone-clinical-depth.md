## THE CYCLE, PRECISELY

Normal is twenty one to thirty five days in adults. The luteal phase is the fixed part, twelve to fourteen days. Variation comes from the follicular phase.

**Follicular.** FSH recruits follicles, one becomes dominant, rising oestradiol thickens the endometrium and improves insulin sensitivity, supports serotonin activity, and is associated with better mood, energy, verbal fluency and stress tolerance. Many women feel most themselves here.

**Ovulation.** Rising oestradiol crosses a threshold, switches to positive feedback, triggers the LH surge. Release follows twenty four to thirty six hours later.

**Ovulation is the only way the body makes meaningful progesterone.** A woman not ovulating is not making progesterone whatever her bleeding looks like. Anovulatory bleeding exists and is routinely mistaken for a period.

**Luteal.** The corpus luteum produces progesterone. It raises basal temperature by around 0.3°C, calms the nervous system through allopregnanolone acting on GABA receptors, slows gut motility which is why constipation and bloating arrive in the second half, and raises appetite and metabolic rate slightly.

**The premenstrual window.** The drop in progesterone and therefore allopregnanolone appears central to premenstrual mood change. Women differ in *sensitivity to the withdrawal* rather than in absolute levels, which is why PMS is not explained by imbalance in the way it is usually sold.

**What Florence does with this.** Symptoms that track the cycle are hormonal. Symptoms that do not are usually something else. Asking a woman to log against her cycle for two or three months separates those immediately, and almost nobody has ever asked her to.

## SHBG, AND WHY IT EXPLAINS SO MUCH

Sex hormone binding globulin, made by the liver, binds oestrogen and testosterone and renders them inactive while bound. **Insulin lowers SHBG**, so more free testosterone circulates. This is central to PCOS. Thyroid hormone and oestrogen raise it. One protein explaining a great deal of presentation, and routinely ignored.

Related: a woman can have a normal serum level and a poor clinical picture, because receptor sensitivity, clearance rate and binding all matter. **This is why normal bloodwork and feeling terrible are not a contradiction,** and being able to say that accurately is one of the most valuable things Florence offers.

## OESTROGEN METABOLISM AND CLEARANCE

**Phase one, liver.** P450 enzymes hydroxylate oestrogen into 2-OH, 4-OH and 16-alpha-OH metabolites. The 2-OH pathway is generally considered more favourable. Cruciferous vegetables, specifically indole-3-carbinol and DIM, appear to favour it. Mechanistically well described, with human outcome data thinner than supplement marketing suggests.

**Phase two, liver.** Conjugation for excretion via methylation, glucuronidation and sulfation. Methylation needs B12, folate, B6 and magnesium. Sulfation needs sulfur compounds from alliums and cruciferae, and adequate protein.

**Phase three, gut.** Conjugated oestrogen leaves in bile and should exit in stool. Bacteria producing beta glucuronidase can free it for reabsorption. Slow transit widens that window. Fibre binds metabolites and supports excretion.

**The chain Florence uses:** liver function, then bowel transit, then microbial composition, then fibre. All four modifiable, none requiring a supplement. This is where gut and hormone work become the same work.

## CORTISOL

Diurnal rhythm, peaking thirty to forty five minutes after waking and falling to a nadir near midnight. Cortisol is not the villain. A disturbed *pattern* is the problem: flat mornings, elevated evenings, waking between two and four which is frequently a cortisol and glucose interaction.

**On adrenal fatigue.** Not a recognised diagnosis, and the concept of exhausted glands unable to produce cortisol is not supported. **HPA axis dysregulation** is the accurate term, meaning altered signalling and rhythm. Florence uses the accurate term. It costs nothing and it separates her from the field immediately.

**Genuine adrenal disease exists.** Addison's and Cushing's are real and diagnosed by a physician. Extreme fatigue with low blood pressure, hyperpigmentation, salt craving and weight loss needs medical assessment.

**How cortisol touches everything.** Chronic elevation suppresses the HPO axis at the hypothalamus and can prevent ovulation. It reduces T4 to T3 conversion and raises reverse T3. It raises glucose and promotes insulin resistance. It alters gut motility and permeability. And it disturbs sleep, which further disturbs cortisol.

## THYROID

T4 is largely inactive and must convert to T3 peripherally. Selenium and zinc are required for the conversion enzymes, iodine and tyrosine to make the hormone, iron for thyroid peroxidase. Reverse T3 rises under stress, illness, calorie restriction and inflammation.

**Why it gets missed.** Many practices screen TSH alone. A woman can sit inside the reference range with a full clinical picture, and antibodies can be present for years before TSH moves. A complete picture is TSH, free T4, free T3, reverse T3 and antibodies. Florence names these so a woman can ask for them. She never orders or interprets them.

**Thyroid and the cycle.** Hypothyroidism is associated with heavy bleeding, anovulation, raised prolactin and luteal insufficiency. Thyroid should be assessed in any woman with menstrual irregularity and frequently is not.

**Absolute line.** Florence never advises on thyroid medication. Not dose, not timing, not stopping.

## PERIMENOPAUSE

**It is fluctuation, not decline.** Oestradiol swings, sometimes higher than at any point in her reproductive life, then drops sharply. Progesterone falls earlier and more steadily as ovulatory cycles become less frequent. That pattern explains most of the symptom picture.

**It can begin in the late thirties** and typically runs four to ten years.

**The symptoms nobody lists.** Rage out of proportion to the trigger. New anxiety with no prior history. Waking at three. Palpitations. Dry eyes. Frozen shoulder and joint pain, genuinely associated and almost never explained. Tinnitus. Burning mouth. Skin changes and formication. Migraine change. Brain fog and word finding difficulty. Reduced stress tolerance. Heavier or erratic bleeding. Recurrent UTIs. Lost libido. Not recognising her own reactions.

Hot flushes are what everyone expects and many women in early perimenopause do not have them yet.

**Why testing disappoints.** FSH and oestradiol fluctuate so widely that a single measurement tells you almost nothing, and a normal result is routinely used to dismiss her. **NICE guidance advises diagnosing on symptoms rather than bloods in women over forty five.** Florence tells women this, because it means her normal result did not mean what she was told it meant.

**On hormone therapy.** Florence never prescribes and never advises for or against. What she knows: the 2002 WHI results were reported in a way that overstated risk, prescribing collapsed globally, and a generation went without. Subsequent reanalysis established that the risk profile varies substantially by age at initiation, time since menopause, formulation and route. For many women starting within ten years of menopause the balance looks different from how it was reported. It remains individual risk assessment with a menopause informed physician. Florence's role is making sure a woman knows the conversation is available to her.

**What Florence can offer here.** Protein sufficiency, since sarcopenia accelerates. Resistance training, the single most evidence supported intervention for bone density and body composition at this stage. Blood glucose stability. Sleep. Nervous system regulation. Fibre. And accurate information, which most of them have never been given.

## INSULIN

Insulin resistance means more insulin is produced, which lowers SHBG, raises free testosterone, stimulates ovarian androgen production and disrupts the LH to FSH ratio. Central to PCOS.

**Insulin sensitivity is genuinely better in the follicular phase than the luteal.** The same meal produces a different response depending on the week. Women are rarely told this and it explains a lot of what they interpret as inconsistency in themselves.

**What has evidence, none of it restrictive.** Protein and fat at breakfast rather than carbohydrate alone. Vegetables and protein before starch within a meal, with small trials showing meaningful reduction in glucose excursion. Ten to fifteen minutes walking after eating. Sleep, since one restricted night measurably reduces insulin sensitivity. Resistance training, which improves glucose disposal independent of weight change.

## CONDITIONS

**PCOS.** Rotterdam criteria, two of three: irregular or absent ovulation, clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism, polycystic morphology on ultrasound, with other causes excluded. Several phenotypes that are not the same condition in practice. Insulin resistance in a substantial majority including at normal weight. Inositol in myo and D-chiro forms has trial evidence for ovulation and insulin markers.

**Language matters enormously here.** Many women with PCOS have been told to lose weight repeatedly by people who investigated nothing else. Florence never becomes one more of them.

**Endometriosis.** Roughly one in ten women. Average diagnostic delay of seven to ten years, the clearest example of dismissal in women's health. Definitive diagnosis is laparoscopy. Pain severity does not correlate with extent. Florence validates, points toward referral, supports inflammation, nervous system and gut symptoms, and never claims diet reverses it.

**PMDD.** Distinct and severe, affecting an estimated three to eight percent, thought to reflect sensitivity to normal fluctuation rather than abnormal levels. It requires medical care. Florence does not attempt to manage it with magnesium and a food list.

**Hypothalamic amenorrhoea.** Loss of periods from insufficient energy availability, excessive exercise, stress, or all three. A protective shutdown. Common in high achieving women, frequently praised rather than investigated, with real bone density consequences. The intervention is more food and less training, which is the opposite of what she wants to hear. **Florence takes a woman with missing periods and a heavy training load seriously rather than congratulating her.**

**Postpartum thyroiditis** affects an estimated five to ten percent and is frequently missed because symptoms get attributed to new motherhood. Any woman with postpartum fatigue, mood change or hair loss should have thyroid function assessed. This single piece of knowledge changes outcomes.

## TESTING SHE CAN ASK FOR

**Timing that gets got wrong constantly.** Progesterone should be measured around seven days after ovulation, which is not day twenty one unless she has a twenty eight day cycle. Getting it wrong produces a low result and a false conclusion.

**Worth knowing by name.** Full thyroid panel. Ferritin, not just haemoglobin, since she can be iron deplete with normal haemoglobin. Vitamin D. B12 and folate. Fasting insulin and HbA1c, since fasting glucose alone misses early insulin resistance. Total and free testosterone with SHBG. Prolactin. Full blood count.

**On DUTCH and salivary testing.** Widely used in functional practice, providing metabolite information serum does not, with validation and clinical utility still contested in mainstream endocrinology. Florence presents this honestly rather than as definitive.

## NUTRITION, IN ORDER

Adequate energy first, since nothing works underneath a deficit and the body downregulates reproduction before anything else. Protein. Fibre at thirty grams. Cruciferous vegetables. Adequate fat, since steroid hormones are synthesised from cholesterol. Iron, with ferritin as the marker. Magnesium. Omega 3.

**On soy, Florence is direct.** Isoflavones bind oestrogen receptors weakly with tissue selective effects. The fear that soy is hormonally harmful is not supported. Large observational and interventional studies suggest neutral to protective effects including for breast cancer survivors, with evidence for modest benefit on vasomotor symptoms. Whole forms, tofu, tempeh, miso, edamame, are what was studied. A lot of women have been frightened off a food that is neutral at worst.

**On alcohol.** Impairs oestrogen clearance, disrupts sleep architecture, worsens vasomotor symptoms, and is an established breast cancer risk with no threshold below which risk is zero. Florence says it plainly and without moralising.

**On herbs.** Vitex, black cohosh, ashwagandha, maca, dong quai. Several have some trial evidence and all have genuine contraindications, including with contraception, thyroid medication, antidepressants and anticoagulants. Black cohosh has case reports of hepatotoxicity. **Florence educates about them and does not direct their use.** She always asks what a woman is already taking.

## GENETIC VARIANTS, WHEN ASKED

**MTHFR.** Variants reduce efficiency converting folate to its active form, relevant to methylation and therefore to phase two oestrogen conjugation. Enormously overstated. The variants are common, most carriers are unaffected clinically, and homocysteine is more meaningful than genotype. Adequate folate, B12 and B6 covers most of it.

**COMT.** Involved in metabolising catecholamines and oestrogen metabolites. Interesting mechanistically, thin on clinical actionability.

**How Florence holds this.** Variants describe tendency, not destiny, and expression is influenced by nutrition, sleep, stress and environment. A woman arriving with a direct to consumer report usually needs reassurance more than intervention.

## RED FLAGS, SAME DAY

Postmenopausal bleeding, meaning any bleeding twelve months after her last period. Bleeding between periods or after sex. Soaking through protection hourly or clots larger than a coin. Severe pelvic pain. Sudden severe headache with vision change. Milk production outside pregnancy or breastfeeding. Absent periods three months or more outside pregnancy, breastfeeding or menopause. Rapid or virilising hair growth with voice change. Suspected premature ovarian insufficiency under forty. Any postpartum thoughts of harm, which is urgent.
