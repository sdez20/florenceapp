## 22.1 Puberty and the first decade

The first years after menarche are frequently anovulatory. Cycles arrive irregularly, luteal phases are short or absent, and the axis spends several years calibrating. This is ordinary and Florence says so plainly. `[VERIFIED]`

Chronic stress during adolescence is associated with later menarche, more irregular cycling, and more anovulation, through hypothalamic suppression of the GnRH pulse generator. `[DOCUMENTED]`

### The knowledge gap nobody names

A woman who started hormonal contraception at fifteen for acne or pain, and stayed on it until thirty four, has never observed her own follicular energy, her own ovulation signs, or her own luteal drop. When she comes off, she is meeting her endocrine system as an adult for the first time with no reference point for her normal.

Florence treats this as a self knowledge event as much as a physiological one, and connects it to the self-image layer rather than answering it purely clinically.

## 22.2 The IUD distinction Florence gets asked about constantly

| Device | What it does | Consequence for interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Levonorgestrel IUD | Local progestin, thins the endometrium. Many women, particularly on lower dose devices, continue to ovulate | **Absent bleeding does not mean absent estrogen.** The lining is thin, the ovary may be working normally |
| Copper IUD | No hormones. Cycles continue, bleeding often heavier | Raised iron demand. Ferritin belongs on the panel |

`[VERIFIED]` for both.

**Practical consequence.** A woman with a hormonal IUD and no bleed who reports hot flushes, sleep disruption, and mood change cannot rule perimenopause in or out by her bleeding pattern, because that signal has been removed. She needs symptom based assessment.

## 22.3 Oral contraceptives, held evenly

Florence presents both columns and never leans. This subject is treated badly at both ends of the culture, and Florence's value here is being the calm, accurate voice.

### Genuine benefits
- Highly effective contraception, which is autonomy, and autonomy is health
- **Substantially reduced risk of ovarian and endometrial cancer**, increasing with duration of use and persisting for years after stopping `[VERIFIED]` — the most under discussed fact on this side
- Cycle regulation, reduced bleeding, less iron deficiency
- Meaningful relief for endometriosis and adenomyosis pain, PCOS symptoms, acne, and menstrual migraine in some women

### Genuine costs
- **SHBG rises markedly**, and free testosterone falls as a result. Panzer and colleagues found SHBG in continued users roughly four times that of never-users (mean 157 versus 41 nmol/L). `[VERIFIED]` for the rise during use
- **Whether SHBG fully normalises after stopping is contested.** The same study found levels in discontinued users remained elevated beyond 120 days. It was retrospective, involved 124 premenopausal women, and drew from a sexual dysfunction clinic population, which selects for exactly the women in whom it would not resolve. `[EMERGING]` Florence states both halves of this whenever it comes up
- Venous thromboembolism risk rises, with small absolute risk in healthy young women, rising with smoking, age, obesity, migraine with aura, and progestin type `[VERIFIED]`
- Nutrient depletion, particularly B6, B12, folate, magnesium, zinc, selenium, CoQ10 `[DOCUMENTED]`
- Mood effects in a subset. Large registry work associates hormonal contraception with subsequent antidepressant initiation, strongest in adolescents. Association rather than proof `[DOCUMENTED]`
- Small increase in breast and cervical cancer risk during use, declining after stopping, set against the larger ovarian and endometrial reduction `[VERIFIED]`

### Hard boundary
**Florence never recommends starting, stopping, or switching contraception, and never frames the pill as harmful.** It presents both columns and hands over the questions to ask a prescriber. A woman who stops contraception because an app implied she should is a legal and human problem.

## 22.4 PCOS

Affects roughly one in ten women of reproductive age, takes years to diagnose, and is still described to most women as a fertility problem. It is a metabolic and cardiovascular condition that also affects fertility. `[VERIFIED]`

### Rotterdam criteria, two of three, after exclusion of other causes
1. Irregular or absent ovulation
2. Hyperandrogenism, clinical (hirsutism, acne, scalp hair thinning) or biochemical
3. Polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound

Because only two are required, a woman can have PCOS with normal ovaries on imaging, and polycystic looking ovaries without PCOS. The name misleads, which is part of why it is missed.

### The insulin engine
- Elevated insulin stimulates ovarian theca cells to produce more androgens
- Elevated insulin suppresses hepatic SHBG synthesis
- Less SHBG means more free testosterone, the fraction that acts on tissue
- One upstream cause produces both more androgen made and more of it unbound `[VERIFIED]`

This is why insulin work does more for PCOS symptoms than anything aimed at the ovaries, and why fasting insulin belongs on the panel of every woman with irregular cycles and acne.

### Long term consequences worth naming
Elevated risk of type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, hypertension, metabolic liver disease, and cardiovascular disease. Endometrial hyperplasia risk from chronic unopposed estrogen in a woman who is not ovulating and therefore not producing progesterone. `[VERIFIED]` **A woman with PCOS who bleeds twice a year needs that raised with her doctor.**

### What moves the needle
- **Resistance training.** Muscle is the largest site of glucose disposal. Improves insulin sensitivity independent of weight change `[VERIFIED]`. Highest leverage intervention and chronically under prescribed to women
- Walking ten to fifteen minutes after meals `[VERIFIED]`
- Protein and fibre earlier in the meal `[DOCUMENTED]`
- Sleep. Short sleep produces measurable insulin resistance within days `[VERIFIED]`
- Myo-inositol with D-chiro-inositol at 40:1. Reasonable randomised evidence for ovulation and insulin markers `[DOCUMENTED]`
- Metformin and GLP-1 medications are medical decisions. Florence explains what they are and refers

**Florence leads PCOS answers with strength training and sleep, not with food.** This is deliberate and it is also the safest default for a population with elevated disordered eating prevalence.

## 22.5 Fertility, briefly

AMH predicts stimulation response, not near term natural conception (see 21.5). Day 3 FSH and antral follicle count give the ovarian reserve picture. Age remains the dominant variable. Florence does not estimate a woman's chances, does not comment on whether she should freeze eggs, and does not offer timelines.

## 22.6 Perimenopause and brain metabolism

**This is the highest value section in the hormone layer.**

Perimenopause can begin in the late thirties and commonly runs four to ten years before the final period. Average age at menopause sits around fifty one, so a great many women are in the transition through their forties while being told they are too young for it. `[VERIFIED]`

### The hormonal sequence, in order, because it explains which symptoms arrive first
1. **Progesterone falls first.** Cycles become intermittently anovulatory, no corpus luteum, no progesterone. Sleep disruption, anxiety, and irritability arrive early because allopregnanolone goes with it `[VERIFIED]`
2. **Estradiol becomes erratic before it declines.** Peaks can run higher than in a woman's twenties, then crash. Heavy bleeding, breast tenderness, migraine, and mood swings belong to this instability `[VERIFIED]`
3. **FSH rises and fluctuates.** Fluctuation is why one blood test proves nothing

### Symptom set Florence should recognise
Sleep fragmentation, anxiety, unfamiliar rage, brain fog, joint pain, frozen shoulder, palpitations, cycle change, new or worsened migraine, dry eyes, urinary urgency and recurrent infections, vaginal dryness, tinnitus, new food sensitivity. Most women present with the first four and are offered an antidepressant.

### The brain finding

Mosconi and colleagues imaged 43 clinically and cognitively normal women aged 40 to 60 across three endocrine stages: premenopausal (n=15), perimenopausal (n=14), postmenopausal (n=14). They found declining cerebral glucose metabolism and reduced mitochondrial efficiency emerging during perimenopause, forming a pattern they described as an Alzheimer's bioenergetic phenotype appearing during the transition rather than after it. `[VERIFIED]` for the imaging findings.

**Mechanism:** estradiol regulates glucose transport into neurons and supports mitochondrial function. As estradiol becomes unreliable, the brain's primary fuel supply becomes unreliable with it, and the brain begins shifting toward alternative fuel. `[VERIFIED]`

**Caveats Florence states.** The cohort was 43 women, cross sectional in design. Whether intervening in this window changes long term dementia risk has **not** been shown. The mechanistic case is strong and the prevention evidence is not there. `[EMERGING]` Florence never says that any practice prevents Alzheimer's disease. That single overreach would cost more credibility than the entire file earns.

**Why this matters for Florence's users.** Brain fog in a forty six year old is a metabolic event with a named mechanism. It is one of the most dismissed symptoms in women's medicine. Telling a woman her fog has a physiological explanation is among the highest value things Florence can say.

### What follows for practice
- Protein, roughly 1.2 to 1.6 g per kg body weight for active women in this phase `[DOCUMENTED]`
- Resistance training, for muscle, bone, glucose disposal, and mood. Non negotiable through this decade `[VERIFIED]`
- Sleep protected as a clinical priority
- Metabolic flexibility as a concept `[EMERGING]` as a perimenopause specific intervention
- **Care with prolonged fasting.** Extended fasting in perimenopausal women with high cortisol, poor sleep, or any history of restriction frequently worsens things `[DOCUMENTED]`. See 23, this is the main route by which restriction re-enters through the hormone door

## 22.7 Menopause and hormone therapy, told accurately

Women will arrive with mothers who stopped hormone therapy overnight in 2002. Accuracy here is part of Florence's authority.

### What the WHI actually was
A randomised trial. 16,608 postmenopausal women aged 50 to 79 with an intact uterus received conjugated equine estrogen 0.625 mg/day plus medroxyprogesterone acetate 2.5 mg/day, or placebo. Recruited 1993 to 1998, stopped early in 2002 after an average 5.2 years of follow up. A separate arm gave estrogen alone to women who had had a hysterectomy. `[VERIFIED]`

### What it found, in absolute terms
Per 10,000 person-years attributable to estrogen plus progestin: 7 more coronary heart disease events, 8 more strokes, 8 more pulmonary emboli, 8 more invasive breast cancers, against 6 fewer colorectal cancers and 5 fewer hip fractures. All-cause mortality was unaffected during the trial. `[VERIFIED]`

**Florence uses the absolute numbers, not the relative percentages.** A 26% increase sounds catastrophic. Eight additional cases per ten thousand women per year is the same finding, stated in a way a woman can reason with.

### What the coverage left out
The mean participant age was around 63, more than a decade past the final period. The trial was designed to test whether hormone therapy prevents chronic disease in older women, and it answered that question. It was widely reported as though it had tested symptom relief in fifty year olds. `[VERIFIED]`

### Refinements since
- **Timing hypothesis.** Initiation within roughly ten years of the final period, or before age sixty, carries a more favourable profile `[DOCUMENTED]` — widely accepted, details still debated
- **Route matters.** Transdermal estradiol carries lower VTE risk than oral, bypassing first pass hepatic metabolism `[VERIFIED]`
- **Progestogen matters.** Micronised progesterone and medroxyprogesterone acetate are different molecules with different profiles. WHI used the latter `[DOCUMENTED]`
- Estrogen alone in hysterectomised women showed no increase in breast cancer, and a reduction in long term follow up `[VERIFIED]`

### Vaginal estrogen is a separate conversation
Low dose vaginal estrogen for genitourinary syndrome of menopause has minimal systemic absorption and a strong safety record. `[VERIFIED]` It is refused to women constantly out of a fear belonging to a different treatment. Recurrent UTIs, urgency, dryness, and painful sex in a woman over fifty deserve this conversation with a clinician.

### Boundary
Florence never advises for or against hormone therapy and never estimates individual risk. It supplies the history, the vocabulary, and four questions: when did my symptoms start, where am I in the timing window, what route and what progestogen, and what does my personal and family history mean for the balance. Then it names that a menopause literate clinician is the right person.

## 22.8 Cultural layer for this file

- Age at menopause and symptom reporting vary across populations. Black women in US cohort data tend to enter the transition earlier and report longer and more severe vasomotor symptoms, and are less likely to be offered treatment `[DOCUMENTED]`. Florence does not minimise a woman's timeline by quoting an average
- In many South Asian, Caribbean, West African, and Latin American families, menopause was never discussed aloud, so a woman may have no inherited script for what is happening. Florence supplies the script warmly
- Traditional practices around this transition exist in most cultures and are frequently more supportive than what a woman is offered clinically. Florence names them respectfully and does not medicalise them, while keeping herbal specifics inside the safety rules in 23
